Logic in computer science

  • Subject codeINBPA0101-21
  • Responsible personDr. Vaszil György
  • Credits6
  • Lessons/week2+2+0
  • TypeCompulsory
  • AssessmentExam

Description

  • Introduction examples. Exploring the logical structure of statements.
  • Formalization in propositional logic. The language of propositional logic, its alphabet. Inductive definition of propositional formulas. The basic element of syntax: degree, (immediate) subformula, precedence of connectives, scope of connectives in a formula.
  • Unary and binary logical operations, truth table. Concepts of semantics: interpretation, truth valuation in interpretations. Satisfiability and validity. Equivalent formulas. Consequences in propositional logic. Natural language reasoning.
  • First-order logic languages. Examples. Inductive definition of terms and formulas. Syntax and semantics. Formalization in first-order logic. Free and bound occurrence of variables. Renaming bound variables, congruent formulas. Interpretations, variable assignment. Term and formula valuation. Satisfiable and valid first-order formulas and the contradiction. Equivalent first-order formulas.
  • Conjunctive and disjunctive normal forms.
  • Prenex form. First-order consequences. Checking the correctness of reasoning.
  • Logical calculus (e.g. sequent calculus), soundness and completeness. Derivations in calculus.
  • First-order logic languages and the programming languages: parallelism, applications, outlook.

Competences

Knowledge. Students absolved this course are expected to know and understand the basic concepts of logic (logic language, syntax, semantics, satisfiability, equivalence, consequence, logical calculus, normal forms, prenex form) and, the basic methods of logic (proof, derivation in calculus, normalization, prenexization).

Abilities. Students absolved this course will be able to determine concepts correctly, to formulate statements, to correctly conclude, to check the correctness of conclusions both via formal and informal way; to use and apply formal logical machinery for information technology problems appearing from the praxis (algorithms, database systems management, logic programming).

Attitude. Students absolved this course are supposed to meet the possibilities and barriers of propositional and first-order logics.

Autonomy, responsibility. The student takes the responsibility concerning the necessary time and learning methods, accepts the requirements of precise and high quality working.

Detailed subject matter

Lecture

Instructors

Dr. Kádek Tamás

Weekly schedule

  1. Basic concept of set theory.

  2. Preliminary introduction of mathematical logic languages.

  3. Syntax: language of the logic. Formulae, connectives, subformulae, syntatic tree.

  4. Semantics, truth tables, interpretation, value of a formula.

  5. Satisfiable, valid and contradictory formulae.

  6. Logical equivalence, CNF, DNF, Karnaugh map for simplification.

  7. A simple logic calculi (sequent calculus) in propositional logic. Soundness and completeness

  8. First order logic: syntax.

  9. First order logic: semantics I.

  10. First order logic: semantics II.

  11. Consequences in first-order logic. Natural language reasoning.

  12. Sequent calculus in first-order logic.

  13. First-order logic languages and the programming languages: parallelism, applications.

Requirements

The requirement for obtaining a signature is to participate in the seminars. More than three absences will result in the refusal of the practical signature.

Exams/Tests

During the semester, four short quizzes will be organized at the time of the seminars, lasting 20-25 minutes.

The result of the short quizzes won't affect the exam grade or the practice signature, except that extra percentage points can be awarded for outstanding performance. Those extra percentage points will be added to the results of the exams organized in the current semester's exam period. After every five percentage points achieved above 80% in the short quizzes, one extra percentage point will be awarded, i.e., one percentage point from 85%, two from 90%, three from 95% and 4 for 100%. This means that 16 extra percentage points can be collected at most during the seminar period.
